## The three tiers

Every position belongs to exactly one tier. The tier is a statement about what
job the money is doing, not about how much you like the company.

### Preservation

**What belongs here.** Money that has to still be there on a specific date, or
that exists to be sellable in a month when everything else is down 30%. Short
and intermediate duration bonds, T-bills, cash equivalents, and equities held
explicitly for stability rather than growth.

**Why it exists.** It is the only tier that can meet a near-term obligation
without forcing a sale at whatever price the market is offering that week. It
is what makes the rest of the portfolio survivable.

**The case against.** It reliably loses to inflation over long horizons, and
holding a large preservation tier for decades is one of the most expensive
mistakes available to a young investor. Every dollar here is a dollar not
compounding. If your near-term needs are small and your horizon is long, a
large preservation tier is not prudence — it is a fear you have not examined.

### Diversified

**What belongs here.** Broad, boring, low-cost exposure. Total-market index
funds, global equity, anything where the thesis is "capitalism generally works
and I do not need to be right about which company."

**Why it exists.** It is the part of the portfolio that does not depend on you
being right. It should be the default, and anything outside it should have to
argue why it beats this.

**The case against.** It guarantees you will never beat the market, and it
quietly imports whatever concentration the index has — a "diversified" total
market fund can be a third of its weight in a handful of very large companies.
Diversified is a description of intent, not a guarantee of the outcome. Check
what is actually inside it.

### Speculative

**What belongs here.** Positions with a specific thesis that could be wrong.
Individual companies, thematic bets, anything where you are claiming to know
something.

**Why it exists.** It is where a written thesis and a written invalidation
condition do actual work. It is also, honestly, where the interest is — and a
portfolio you find boring is one you will stop maintaining.

**The case against.** Most of these will underperform the diversified tier
after costs and taxes, and you will remember the winners and forget the
losers. This tier grows on you when it wins, which is exactly when you least
want to notice — that is what the `speculativeCreep` rule is for. Size it as
if you will be wrong about most of it, because you will be.

## The rules that enforce this

The thresholds live in `lib/constants.ts` and nowhere else. Change them there.

| Rule | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| `allocationDrift` | any tier is ≥5 points off its target |
| `concentration` | any single position is ≥25% of the portfolio |
| `undocumented` | any position has an empty thesis or invalidation |
| `stalePrice` | a price is older than 14 days |
| `drawdownReview` | a position is ≥25% below cost basis |
| `speculativeCreep` | the speculative tier is >40% of the portfolio |
| `horizonMismatch` | near-term needs exceed cash plus the preservation tier |
| `thinPortfolio` | there are fewer than 3 open positions |

## What this app will not do

It has no brokerage connection and cannot place an order. It does not produce
buy or sell signals. Rules produce flags, flags produce questions, and the
answers are yours. Every AI-generated brief is stored permanently, including
the ones that turn out to be wrong, so that in six months you can count.
